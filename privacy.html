Privacy Policy

Birdies & Bets: Golf Scorecard · Effective 27 October 2026

Birdies & Bets is a golf scorecard app that keeps score and tracks the side games a group agrees to play. An account is optional. Without one, everything you enter stays on your phone. If you sign in, your rounds are kept in your account so they follow you to any phone. Either way, what you enter is saved on your phone first, and a signed-in phone syncs it to your account when it has a connection. An account also lets you add friends, share a round so everyone scores on their own phone, and watch friends play. Friends and shared rounds explains who sees what.

This policy covers the Birdies & Bets app and this website, www.birdiesandbets.com.

Who we are

Birdies & Bets is made and run by St. Margaret’s Ventures Inc., a company based in Ontario, Canada, which is responsible for the personal information described here (the “controller” under data protection laws such as the GDPR). In this policy, “we” and “us” mean St. Margaret’s Ventures Inc. You can reach us about anything in this policy at hello@birdiesandbets.com.

What we collect

Only what the app needs to keep your golf with you. There is no advertising, we never sell data, and the app does not track you across other companies' apps or websites. If you use the app without an account, none of the items below leaves your phone: they are kept in the app's private storage, and in a backup file only if you make one.

  • Your account, if you make one. When you sign in with Apple we receive a private account identifier from Apple, and your name and email only if you choose to share them (Apple lets you hide your email behind a relay address). When you sign in by email we receive your email address, used only to send your sign-in codes.
  • Your name and handicap index, as you want them shown on scorecards and to your friends.
  • Your golf. The rounds you play and score, your round history, the players you add (their names and handicap indexes, as you enter them), courses you create or import, and the round settings and preferences that should follow you between phones (what points are called, the handicap allowance, your home course, and the bag and home area that older versions asked for).
  • Friends, if you use them. Your username, your friends and friend requests, the people you block, the invite links you make, and the reports you send (who you reported, the reason and when).
  • Shared rounds you play in. The course and players, every score with when it was entered and by which account, game answers, hole notes and the round's settings. See Friends and shared rounds.
  • Push tokens. If you allow notifications, a token for each phone so we can send them, and the notifications we send you. See Notifications.

With an account, we use this only to run the app: to sign you in, to keep your data in sync between your phones, to bring it back if you reinstall or change phones, to run the friends features you use, and to review reports. Device-only settings (haptics, your privacy choices, your profile photo) stay on the phone.

Purchases. If you buy a subscription or other in-app purchase, it is paid through Apple's App Store. Apple handles the payment, and we never receive your card or payment details: the app only learns which purchases are active, so it can unlock the features you paid for.

Two more things can leave your phone, with or without an account, and neither is linked to you: crash reports, which are on unless you turn them off, and usage counts, which are off unless you turn them on. Both are under Profile → Settings → Privacy.

Where it is kept

If you sign in, your account and synced data are stored with Supabase (operated by Supabase, Inc.), our database provider, protected so that only your signed-in account can read or change it. Your friends list, shared rounds, reports and push tokens are stored there too. What friends and other players can see is set out under Friends and shared rounds. A copy also stays in the app's private storage on your phone. Signing out removes that copy from the phone; your account keeps it. Without an account, your data exists only on your phone (and in any backup file you save), so deleting the app deletes it.

Why we use it

Where data protection laws such as the GDPR ask for a legal reason to use personal information, ours are:

  • To provide the app you asked for (performing our agreement with you): your account, sign-in, syncing and restoring your golf, and the friends features you choose to use: your username, friends, shared rounds and notifications.
  • Your consent: usage analytics, which are off unless you turn them on. You can withdraw consent at any time in Settings → Privacy.
  • Our legitimate interest in a working, secure app: crash reports (which you can turn off), course search, update checks, and the routine server logs our providers keep to run their services and stop abuse.
  • Our legitimate interest in keeping the app safe for the people who use it: storing and reviewing reports, and acting on them, for example by removing a username or an account that breaks our terms.
  • Answering you: if you email us, we use your email address and message to reply.
  • Early access, with your consent: if you ask for early access, we use your email address only to send you launch news and test invitations for Birdies & Bets. Every one of these emails has an unsubscribe link, or you can reply and ask us to stop.

How long we keep it

  • Account and synced golf: for as long as you have an account. When you delete your account it is removed from our database straight away, along with the records of your sign-ins; copies in our database provider's routine backups are overwritten as those backups expire.
  • Data on your phone: until you delete it, sign out, or delete the app. We cannot see it.
  • Shared rounds: deleted from our servers by a daily cleanup 30 days after the round ends, or 30 days after it started if it never finished. Each player who joined keeps their own copy in their round history.
  • Friendships, friend requests and blocks: until they are ended in the app (removing a friend, declining or cancelling a request, unblocking) or either account is deleted.
  • Invite links: each works once and for 7 days. The record of a link is kept until you delete your account; used and expired links may be cleared sooner.
  • Reports: kept until we have dealt with them. If an account named in a report is deleted first, the report stays without that account.
  • Push tokens: until you sign out on that phone or delete your account, or until the push service tells us the token no longer works (for example, after you delete the app).
  • Notifications: deleted from our servers after 7 days.
  • Crash reports: deleted automatically within 90 days.
  • Usage counts: not linked to you or your phone, so they cannot be traced back to you or deleted per person.
  • Emails to us: kept as long as needed to deal with your question, then deleted.
  • Early-access requests: kept until the app launches, then deleted unless you have written to us about something else.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account at any time in the app under Profile → Settings → Account → Delete account. That permanently deletes your account and everything stored with it from our database, and clears it from the phone. If you signed in with Apple, we also revoke the app's access to your Apple ID. You can also email us to ask for deletion; Delete your data explains both ways and exactly what goes. If you only want to stop using one phone, sign out instead.

Deleting your account also deletes your username, friendships, friend requests, blocks, invite links and push tokens, and takes you out of every shared round. A shared round that other players with accounts are still in keeps its scores for them, including your name as a player as it was entered, until it is deleted 30 days after it ends. A shared round with no player with an account left in it is deleted straight away. Reports you sent, or that name you, stay until we have dealt with them, without your account.

Friends and shared rounds

Friends and shared rounds need an account. Without one, they do nothing.

Usernames and friend requests. You can choose a username: 3 to 20 lowercase letters, numbers or _. Reserved and offensive names are refused. Other people can find you only by typing your exact username: there is no directory to browse and no partial search. They can then send you a friend request, which you accept or decline. You can also share an invite link (www.birdiesandbets.com/friend#…). Each link works once and for 7 days, and whoever opens it and accepts becomes your friend. The invite is in the part of the link after the #, which web browsers never send to a server, so our website never receives it. The app reads it and checks it with our database.

What other people see.

  • Anyone with an account who types your exact username sees your name and username, so they can send you a request. So does anyone who opens an invite link you sent.
  • People with a pending friend request, sent or received, see your name and username.
  • Your friends also see your handicap index, and the shared rounds described below.
  • People you block can no longer find you or add you, you stop being friends, and they can't watch any shared round you are in, even through another player they are friends with. They are not told. You can also remove a friend at any time.

A friend with an account is linked to the player saved on your phone, so their name and handicap index come from their account. In the same way, the app copies your name and handicap index to your account so your friends see them.

Shared rounds. When you are signed in, a round you start is shared from the start if Share live rounds with friends is on (it is, unless you turn it off), or if you put a friend with an account in it. Friends you put in the round get it on their phone, tap Join and score on their own phone, and every phone's card stays in step. For a shared round, our servers (Supabase) store the course and tees; the players, with their names and handicap indexes as entered and the account of each player who has one; every score, with when it was entered and which account entered it; game answers and hole notes; the games and round settings; and who joined. When the round finishes, each player's phone files its own copy in that player's round history, which syncs to their account like any round.

Who can see a shared round:

  • The players in it who have accounts, while it is played and afterwards, until they leave it.
  • Friends of a player in it, if that player has joined the round and has Share live rounds with friends on: while it is played (up to 18 hours from the start) and for 2 hours after it finishes. They see the whole round as stored, including every player's name, handicap index and scores, the games and the hole notes. This includes friends of the other players, who may be people you don't know.

Turning off Share live rounds with friends (Profile → Settings → Friends) stops your friends watching through you, including in a round you are playing now. Friends of other players who share can still watch a round you are in. Any player can leave a round, and whoever started it can end it for everyone; each player keeps the scores on their own phone. The Friends tab lists the rounds you can watch under Playing now and opens a read-only card that updates as your friends score. While you are signed in and the app is open, it keeps a live connection to Supabase so friend requests, rounds you are added to and new scores arrive as they happen.

Reports. You can report someone from a friend's page or a friend request, choosing a reason: an offensive name or username, spam or a fake account, or something else. The report is stored on our servers with who reported whom, the reason, when, and the round it is about if there is one, so we can review it. We act on reports, for example by removing an offending username or an account that breaks our terms.

Notifications

Notifications need an account. The app asks once whether to allow them, after your first friend request, invite or shared round. If you allow them, the app stores a push token for your phone with your account, so our server can send to it. An account keeps tokens for at most 10 phones.

We send a notification when someone sends you a friend request, when your request or invite link is accepted, when a friend adds you to a round, and when a friend who shares their rounds starts or finishes one. Our server sends each one through Expo's push service (operated by Expo / 650 Industries) and the Apple Push Notification service (operated by Apple Inc.), which deliver it to your phone. They receive your phone's push token and the notification's text, such as “Marcus wants to be friends.” or “Cody added you to a round at Cedar Ridge.”, which names the other person and sometimes a course. Sent notifications are deleted from our servers after 7 days.

You can turn off notifications about friends' rounds starting and finishing under Profile → Settings → Notifications → Friends' rounds, and all of them in iOS Settings. Signing out removes the phone's push token from your account when the phone has a connection, and deleting your account deletes it.

Shared scorecards

When you share a finished scorecard, the app sends the picture of the card along with a link to it. The link itself carries what is on that card: the course and tee names, the date, the hole pars, each player's name, scores and handicap strokes, and the game results. That information sits in the part of the link after the #, which web browsers never send to a server, so our website never receives it. The page at www.birdiesandbets.com/card reads it inside the recipient's browser, or the app opens it directly. Nothing from a shared card is stored by us. Anyone who has the link can see the card, so share it with the people you played with.

Network requests the app makes

  • Update checks. On launch the app may ask Expo's update service (EAS Update, operated by Expo / 650 Industries) whether a newer version of the app's code is available. That request carries the app version, platform and runtime identifier, a random ID for this installation of the app and, if the app crashed on its last launch, that error message. Expo's servers also see your IP address. It carries no scores, names or courses.
  • Course search. When you type in the course search box, the text you type is sent to our course lookup service (a Cloudflare Worker we operate) which forwards it to GolfCourseAPI to find scorecards. Those services see the search text and your IP address. Our lookup service keeps your IP address for up to a minute to limit abuse, and keeps search results for up to 7 days, stored by the search text and not linked to you, so repeat searches are fast. Nothing else from the app is sent. Courses you download are stored on your phone; saved courses and manual entry work without any connection.
  • Connection checks. While you are signed in, the app checks about once a minute whether it is online, so it knows when it can sync, by sending an empty request to Google (clients3.google.com, operated by Google LLC). Google sees your IP address; the request carries nothing about you or your golf.

Crash reports

When the app hits an error, it can send a crash report to Sentry (operated by Functional Software, Inc.) so we can find and fix the bug. Crash reports are on by default, and you can turn them off at any time under Settings → Privacy → Crash reports. Nothing is sent before your settings have loaded.

A crash report contains the error message and where in the app's code it happened, a short log of what the app was doing just before it, your phone model, iOS version, app version and update ID, and general device details such as screen size, memory, language and time zone. Before a report leaves your phone the app removes your device's name, and it never includes player names, scores, courses, notes or anything you typed. The report carries no account, advertising ID or other identifier for you. Sentry's servers see your IP address when the report arrives; we do not store it with the report. Reports are deleted automatically within 90 days.

Usage analytics

If you say yes, the app sends usage counts, not linked to you, to Aptabase, a privacy-first analytics service, so we can see which features get used. Usage analytics are off unless you turn them on, either when the app first asks or under Settings → Privacy → Usage analytics, and you can turn them off at any time.

The only events are: a round started (how many holes and players, which games, shotgun start or not), a round finished (how many holes were scored, and which games), a round discarded (how many holes were scored), a course imported or created, a scorecard shared, and a backup saved or restored. Each event also carries your iOS version, app version and build number, a language code, and a random session number that changes after an hour without use. There are no names, scores, course names, locations, device identifiers or advertising IDs. Aptabase does not use cookies or fingerprinting and does not store IP addresses.

This website

www.birdiesandbets.com has no accounts, forms, cookies, ads or analytics, and stores nothing in your browser. Its fonts and code are served from the site itself, so your browser contacts no other company to show it. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc.), which, like any web host, sees your IP address and browser details and may log them to run and secure the service, under GitHub's own privacy policy. A shared scorecard page loads the same way, and the card's contents stay in your browser. A friend invite page loads the same way and does not read the invite.

Where your information is processed

We are based in Canada. We and the providers named in this policy may process your information in Canada, the United States and other countries whose data protection laws can differ from yours. Where the law requires it, these transfers rely on safeguards such as the European Commission's standard contractual clauses that the providers offer.

Security

Everything the app and this website send travels over encrypted connections (HTTPS). Synced data is protected so that only your signed-in account can read or change it, apart from what friends and other players can see as described under Friends and shared rounds. The app collects as little as it can in the first place. No system is perfectly secure; if a breach ever affects your personal information, we will tell you and the authorities as the law requires.

Money

The app never holds or moves money between players. It shows a tally of points and provides no way to pay, deposit, withdraw, or transfer anything between players. The only payments are for the app itself, such as a subscription, and those go through Apple (see Purchases).

Children

Birdies & Bets is for people 13 and older, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has made an account, email us and we will delete it. If you are under 16, or under the age of digital consent where you live (14 in Quebec, for example), ask a parent or guardian before turning on usage analytics.

Children as players. Younger golfers can still be on the card: an adult can add them as a player in their own round. Their name and handicap are then stored with that adult's rounds like any other player's. In a shared round, the other players and the friends who watch it can see them too. Only add a child with their parent's or guardian's OK. We never contact players or use their details for anything except your scorecards, and a parent or guardian can email us to have a child's details deleted.

Crash reports and usage counts are not linked to the person using the app and contain no names, scores or courses.
